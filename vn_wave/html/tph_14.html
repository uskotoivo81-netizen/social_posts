To check if a page is indexed on Google, use the site: search operator, Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool, or a bulk index checker for many URLs at once. Each method answers the same question: has Google added this URL to its index so it can appear in search results?

Quick checks for one URL

The fastest way to check if a page is indexed on Google is the site: operator. Type site:yourdomain.com/your-page into Google. If the page appears in results, it is indexed. If nothing shows up, Google has not indexed it yet.

Learn the site: operator limits before relying on it. It works well for individual pages but becomes impractical for checking hundreds of backlinks, migrated pages, or newly published content. Google also sometimes omits results from site: queries, especially for URLs with parameters or canonical issues, so treat a missing result as a signal to investigate rather than a final verdict.

For your own website, URL Inspection in Google Search Console gives a more authoritative answer. Paste the URL, and Google shows whether it is indexed, when it was last crawled, and any indexing issues. The catch is that GSC only works for properties you own, and its data can lag behind reality.

Bulk checks for many URLs

When you need to verify dozens, hundreds, or thousands of URLs — backlinks from guest posts, tier-2 links, or pages after a site migration — manual checking does not scale. You cannot run site: queries for 10,000 pages, and you cannot check backlinks in GSC because you do not own those domains.

This is where a bulk checker such as vn.speedyindex.com/kiem-tra-index-google/ helps. It queries Google directly and returns a clear "indexed" or "not indexed" status for each URL without ownership restrictions. You can check up to 100,000 URLs per task, which is useful for auditing backlink campaigns or diagnosing pages that Search Console marks as "Crawled – currently not indexed."

FAQ

What does the site: operator actually show?

The site: operator shows pages from a specific domain or URL that Google has indexed and is willing to display in search results. It is a quick manual check, but it is not a complete inventory of every indexed page.

Can you check index status for pages you do not own?

Yes, but not through Google Search Console. You need a third-party index checker that queries Google directly and reports the status for any URL, regardless of ownership. This is essential for backlink audits.

Why does GSC say "Discovered – currently not indexed"?

This status means Google has found the URL but has not crawled and indexed it yet. Common reasons include low content quality, weak internal linking, or crawl budget limits on large sites.
